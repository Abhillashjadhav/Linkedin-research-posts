# Incident → Damage → Solution strategy

## Purpose

The strongest default hook for authority posts is a verified real-world incident with a concrete consequence, followed by the product mechanism that failed and the bounded solution Abhillash has built or recommends.

## Preferred sequence

```text
Verified incident
→ Verified damage or consequence
→ Failed product or governance mechanism
→ What Abhillash built or would change
→ How that intervention reduces recurrence, cost, or exposure
```

## Required evidence

An incident-led post may proceed only when the research package supports:

1. what happened;
2. when it happened;
3. who was affected;
4. the actual consequence;
5. any number, scale, cost, duration, or customer effect used in the hook;
6. the mechanism or decision failure;
7. a natural connection to an approved proof item or product decision.

A quantified damage claim requires a primary source or corroboration from two reputable independent sources.

## Hook standard

The opening should normally name the incident and consequence within the first two lines. It should create tension through facts, not adjectives.

Good:

> McDonald’s tested AI ordering across more than 100 drive-thrus. The pilot ended after repeated ordering failures became public.

Bad:

> AI is changing everything, but most companies are not ready.

## Bounded solution language

Allowed:

- would have exposed the risk earlier;
- reduces the chance of repeating this failure;
- creates a stop condition before investment expands;
- limits the blast radius;
- makes the missing control explicit.

Not allowed without evidence:

- would have prevented the incident;
- would have saved $X;
- guarantees safety;
- eliminates the failure;
- caused reputational damage, unless a source establishes it.

## Selection rule

Prefer this pattern when the incident is current, consequential, relevant to AI product leaders, and naturally connected to Abhillash’s proof of work. Do not force it onto Monday skill posts when no factual incident supports the skill. In that case, use a concrete operator problem, measured workflow cost, or verified failure pattern.

## Editorial test

Before approval, answer yes to all:

- Is the incident real and body-read?
- Is the consequence factual rather than inferred?
- Does the hook contain the most compelling supported detail?
- Is the failed mechanism clear?
- Does the solution bridge feel natural rather than promotional?
- Is the counterfactual claim bounded?
- Would the post still be useful if the company name were removed?
